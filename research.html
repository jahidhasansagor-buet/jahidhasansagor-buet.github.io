Research

Predicting heat transfer where classical models fail — from quantum effects at sub-nanometer gaps to material properties at extreme temperatures.

The problem

When two surfaces are brought within nanometers of each other, radiative heat transfer can exceed the classical blackbody limit by orders of magnitude. At sub-nanometer separations, even fluctuational electrodynamics stops being sufficient, and a full quantum-mechanical treatment is required. At the same time, most thermal analyses use room-temperature material properties — even for systems operating at hundreds or thousands of kelvin.

My doctoral work addresses both gaps. Using the non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) formalism and first-principles calculations, I model heat transfer in the extreme near-field regime with material properties computed as functions of temperature. One key result: using room-temperature properties can underpredict thermal conductance by 32–43% at 1200 K — a significant error for the design of high-temperature energy systems.

What I work on

Extreme near-field heat transfer

Modeling phonon and photon thermal transport across sub-nanometer vacuum gaps using the NEGF formalism and fluctuational electrodynamics.

Temperature-dependent optical properties

First-principles dielectric functions of graphene, silicon, and gold at finite temperature, computed with Quantum ESPRESSO and Yambo using density functional theory and many-body perturbation theory.

Phonons and anharmonicity

Capturing how lattice vibrations reshape optical response at finite temperature, including anharmonic effects through special-displacement methods.

Validation and high-performance computing

Large-scale ab initio simulations on Purdue's Anvil supercomputer and University of Maine clusters, with model predictions validated against published experimental measurements.

Earlier research: computational fluid dynamics

At BUET, working with Dr. Sumon Saha, I performed CFD simulations of single-phase convective, conjugate, and natural/mixed convection heat transfer in cavity-based systems — rotating cylinders, heat-generating bodies, nanofluids, and coupled solid–fluid domains. I solved the Navier–Stokes and energy equations with the finite element method, ran mesh sensitivity and verification studies, and analyzed velocity fields, temperature distributions, and Nusselt numbers. This work produced two Q1 journal papers and four conference papers, and I mentored undergraduate researchers through the full CFD workflow.
